EVIDENCE An evidence review · 1996–2026

The case for nature, in one place.

Nature's effect on our health and well-being is real, and measurable, which is exactly why equitable, nearby access matters. We gather that evidence, help advance it through research we have funded ourselves, and continue to place it in the hands of the people who need it: insight and data a community can use to protect the greenspace it has, and to make the case for what it doesn't yet have.

Measure i · The field, in brief Five findings · peer-reviewed01 / 07
Fig. 1 Dose & response

The first minutes do the most work

—which is a core argument for nearby, everyday greenspace.

Early studies asked for hours in wilderness. Today, we know that as little as 10 minutes in a place where you feel connected to nature produces measurable physiological changes including a drop in cortisol, and a drop in blood pressure. Longer is better still; the point is that you don't have to wait for longer to benefit.

Synthesis of published dose–response findings on nature contact and stress biomarkers.

Thirty years of unprompted testimony.

Why this matters: it's the evidence only we have.

Beneath every Nature Sacred bench sits a blank, waterproof journal. No question is asked of visitors, and nothing is collected from them. What they write is anonymous and voluntary. Read across decades and hundreds of sites, it is remarkably consistent.

Wildcat Way at César Chávez Elementary School

Measure iv · The Network reports back A standing feedback loop05 / 07

The people who tend Sacred Places tell us what the data can't.

Why this matters: Firesouls see what the studies can’t capture in real time.

Every Sacred Place has a Firesoul—the local champion who cares for it. They are on the ground every week, and they report back to us continuously. That loop is the fourth source of our evidence, and the one that keeps the other three honest.
